# CareQueue go-to-market and onboarding

## Beachhead

One city, one sub-vertical first: **busy GP and paediatric clinics** (30–120 patients a day, visible crowd). Queue pain is obvious and the owner doctor decides alone. Add dental and physio (appointment and session reminders) and small labs (report delivery) from month 4.

Why not labs first: lab chains already have LIS and WhatsApp delivery; independent labs are a good second wave once report delivery is proven.

## Getting the first 10 paying customers (weeks 6–12)

| Step | Action | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | List 80 clinics in 2–3 neighbourhoods from Google Maps: 50+ reviews, reviews mentioning "wait", "crowd", "rush" | 80 clinics |
| 2 | Visit the receptionist first during OPD (see the crowd), leave a one-page card; book 10 minutes with the doctor before or after OPD | 40 doctor meetings |
| 3 | Demo on the doctor's phone: scan QR, get token, see live wait, receive "your turn" WhatsApp, see a sample e-Rx link | 30 demos |
| 4 | Offer **Founding Clinic deal**: 60 days free on Clinic tier, then ₹999/month locked for 12 months, free QR kit and TV setup. Condition: feedback + permission to cite results. | 12 pilots |
| 5 | Install during next OPD session; stay 1 hour to coach receptionist and patients | 12 live |
| 6 | Day 3, 7, 14 check-ins; day 21 show numbers: tokens issued, average wait, follow-ups set | 11 active |
| 7 | Day 45–60: convert; UPI autopay or annual | 10 paying |
| 8 | Ask each doctor for 2 colleague introductions (batchmates, local IMA/IDA/IAP branch WhatsApp groups) | 20 warm leads |

Founder time: about 5 weeks. Cash: kits ≈ ₹4,000, travel ≈ ₹6,000 (estimate).

| Channel | How | Expected CAC (estimate) | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Doctor referrals | 1 month free for both | ₹1,600 | From customer 10 |
| Field reps | Rep visits clinics before/after OPD hours; 6 closes a month | ₹6,000 | Month 5+ |
| Local medical associations (IMA, IDA, IAP, physio associations) | Sponsor a CME tea break, 10-minute talk on "DPDP for clinics" | ₹3,000–₹5,000 | Month 4+ |
| Lab partners | Independent labs give CareQueue to referring doctors; labs get report delivery | ₹2,500 | Month 6+ |
| Medical equipment and pharmacy distributors | Referral fee ₹1,000 per activated clinic | ₹2,000 | Month 6+ |
| Content (YouTube/Instagram for doctors, Hindi + English) | "How Dr. X cut his waiting room by half", "DPDP checklist for clinics" | ₹3,000–₹5,000 | Month 5+ |
| Patient-side virality | "Token by CareQueue" on every patient token page | Low | Always |

Avoid pharma-sponsored distribution. It creates conflicts of interest and data-sharing pressure.

## Sales script (doctor, 3 minutes)

1. **Open with what you saw.** "Doctor, aapke waiting room mein abhi 25 log baithe the. Unme se kitne poochte hain ki kitna time lagega?" (There were 25 people in your waiting room. How many ask how long it will take?)
2. **Show.** "Ye QR scan kijiye." Doctor gets token, live wait, then a "your turn" WhatsApp.
3. **Patient benefit.** "Patient bahar chai pi sakta hai, ghar pe ruk sakta hai. Bheed aadhi, shor khatam." (Patients wait outside or at home. Half the crowd, no noise.)
4. **Money line.** "Aap jo follow-up date likhte hain, uska reminder apne aap jaata hai. Roz 3 patient bhi wapas aaye to mahine ka ₹30,000." (Every follow-up you set gets a reminder. Three returning patients a day is ₹30,000 a month.)
5. **Trust line.** "Data aapka hai. Patient khud consent deta hai, sab encrypted, India mein stored. Koi medical advice system nahi deta." (Data belongs to you. Patients consent themselves, encrypted, stored in India. The system gives no medical advice.)
6. **Close.** "60 din free. Kal OPD se pehle QR laga dete hain." (60 days free. We install the QR before tomorrow's OPD.)

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "My patients are old, they will not scan." | "Receptionist can issue the token in the app for them; they still get a WhatsApp. Younger family members scan for them." |
| "I already use Practo Ray / an EMR." | "Keep it. CareQueue handles the waiting room and WhatsApp. Your prescriptions can stay in your EMR." |
| "Government eSushrut is cheaper." | "eSushrut is for records. CareQueue is for the crowd at your door and follow-ups on WhatsApp. They work together." |
| "Patient data risk / DPDP." | "Patients consent on their own phone, links are OTP-protected and expire, data is encrypted in India, and we sign a data processing agreement with you." |
| "WhatsApp for health info is unsafe." | "We never send the report or prescription as a file. We send a secure link that needs an OTP." |
| "Too expensive." | "₹1,499 is 4 consultations a month. Try 60 days free and look at the follow-up numbers." |
| "Receptionist will not change." | "She gets fewer calls. We train her during OPD, on site." |
| "Will this give medical advice to patients?" | "No. It only handles tokens, bookings, reminders and documents you approve." |

## Onboarding checklist (target: live in one OPD session)

### Day 0 (on site, 45–60 minutes)

- [ ] Create clinic: name, type, address, OPD timings, languages.
- [ ] Add practitioners with qualification and State Medical Council registration number (shown on e-Rx).
- [ ] Create counters (doctor queues, sample collection, billing) and average consultation time.
- [ ] Sign data processing agreement (clinic = data fiduciary, CareQueue = processor).
- [ ] Configure consent notice language and options; set message wording for sensitive specialities if needed.
- [ ] Connect WhatsApp: clinic's own number via BSP, or CareQueue shared sender while verification completes.
- [ ] Approve utility templates: token, turn alert, appointment reminder, e-Rx link, report ready, follow-up.
- [ ] Create e-Rx templates from the doctor's 10 most common prescriptions (doctor types; we do not suggest).
- [ ] Place QR standee at reception and posters in waiting area; set up TV display if bought.
- [ ] Receptionist issues 5 live tokens; doctor calls next 5 times on the staff app.
- [ ] Set roles: receptionist cannot see prescriptions or notes.

### Day 1–14

- [ ] Day 1: review first OPD; fix average consultation time so live wait is accurate.
- [ ] Day 3: share of tokens via CareQueue ≥ 50%; if not, coach receptionist on site.
- [ ] Day 7: share ≥ 70%; paper tokens stopped except for fallback.
- [ ] Day 10: doctor sending e-Rx and follow-up dates for at least 30% of visits.
- [ ] Day 14: dashboard review with doctor: tokens, average wait, follow-ups set, reviews gained.

### Day 15–60

- [ ] Day 21: first follow-up reminders show returns.
- [ ] Day 30: offer annual with free kit and TV setup.
- [ ] Day 45–60: convert; set up UPI autopay.
- [ ] P1 clinics on Plus: start ABDM HFR/HPR registration and M1 integration.

## Activation definition

A clinic is **activated** when, within 7 days, at least 70% of tokens go through CareQueue and 80% of those have a phone number with consent.

## Retention playbook

| Signal | Action |
|---|---|
| Token share drops below 60% | Visit during OPD; check QR placement, receptionist turnover, internet |
| Live wait estimate inaccurate (patient complaints) | Recalibrate average consult time per practitioner and time of day |
| e-Rx usage below 20% after 30 days | Help doctor build templates; show patient feedback |
| Follow-up reminders unused | Show doctor lapsed follow-ups list and missed revenue |
| Owner has not opened dashboard in 14 days | Monthly WhatsApp report card still goes: patients served, wait trend, follow-ups returned |
| Annual renewal in 30 days | Year-in-review: patients served, hours of waiting saved (tokens × average wait reduction, estimate), reviews gained |
| Any data or privacy question | Same-day response from a named person; publish a security page |

The monthly report card is the main churn defence: it shows the doctor the crowd that did not happen.
